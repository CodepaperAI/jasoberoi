# Luxury Home Construction Cost in Vancouver, BC (2026 Price Guide)

Luxury residential construction is the one build type where square footage tells you the least about the price. A 4,000 sq ft house can be built for a figure that would not cover the millwork and glazing on another house of exactly the same size. The variable is the finish schedule and the structural ambition behind it, not the floor plan. This guide gives the band we build to in Vancouver and the Lower Mainland, what a real project totals at three common sizes, and the five decisions that actually move the number.

> **Key takeaway.** Budget **$300–$400 per sq ft** for luxury home construction in Vancouver — roughly $900,000–$1,200,000 for a 3,000 sq ft home, $1,350,000–$1,800,000 for a 4,500 sq ft home, and $1,800,000–$2,400,000 for a 6,000 sq ft home. That is construction only. Land, design fees, landscaping and furniture sit outside it, and on a west-side lot the land is often the larger of the two numbers.

## Luxury Home Construction Cost by Size

These figures apply the band published on our [luxury residential construction](/services/luxury-residential-construction/) service page. They cover the build — excavation and foundation, structure, envelope, mechanical and electrical, interior finish, millwork and site works — and exclude the items listed further down.

| Home size | Typical scope at this size | Construction cost | Typical construction time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3,000 sq ft | 4 bed, principal suite, single-level garage | $900,000 – $1,200,000 | 12–16 months |
| 4,500 sq ft | 5 bed, media or wellness room, feature stair | $1,350,000 – $1,800,000 | 14–20 months |
| 6,000 sq ft | Full lower level, pool or spa provision, elevator | $1,800,000 – $2,400,000 | 18–26 months |

The low end of each range is a high-quality build with a disciplined finish schedule. The high end is engineered glazing, stone in the primary spaces and joinery drawn for the house rather than ordered for it. Most projects that arrive with a clear brief land nearer the middle.

## Where the Money Actually Goes

The allocation below is what we see across high-finish residential work. It is worth reading before you cut anything, because the two lines owners most often try to trim are the two that are hardest to add back later.

| Package | Share of construction budget | Why it lands where it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure, excavation and foundation | 18–24% | Slope, soil and how much of the house is below grade |
| Envelope — cladding, roofing, waterproofing | 12–16% | Assembly complexity and the number of transitions |
| Windows, doors and glazing | 8–14% | Engineered and oversized units carry structural coordination |
| Mechanical, electrical, plumbing | 14–18% | Radiant heat, ventilation, zoning and control systems |
| Interior finish and tile | 12–16% | Stone selection and the amount of it |
| Millwork and joinery | 10–16% | Shop-drawn cabinetry is the single most elastic line |
| Site works and hard landscaping | 4–8% | Driveways, retaining, drainage |

Millwork and glazing together can swing 20% of the budget on their own. That is the honest answer to why the band is $100 per sq ft wide.

## The Five Decisions That Move the Number Most

**1. The site, before the house.** A flat, serviced lot and a sloped lot with a retaining requirement are two different projects at the same square footage. Excavation, shoring, drainage and access all price off the ground, and none of it appears in the finished house. Get a geotechnical report before you set a budget, not after.

**2. How much of the house is below grade.** A full finished lower level is usually the cheapest square footage in a luxury home per foot, and the most expensive to get wrong. Waterproofing, drainage and headroom decisions made at foundation stage cannot be revisited.

**3. Glazing strategy.** Large-format and corner glazing is the defining feature of most contemporary Vancouver homes and it is never only a window order. Oversized units bring structural headers, thermal-bridging detail, crane access and lead times measured in months.

**4. Millwork scope.** The gap between supplied cabinetry and drawn joinery is the widest single gap in the whole budget. It is also the most visible thing in the finished house, which is why it is a poor candidate for late value engineering.

**5. Mechanical ambition.** Radiant floors, heat recovery ventilation, zoned climate control, integrated lighting and home automation compound. Each is reasonable alone; specified together they move the mechanical and electrical share several points.

## What the Band Does Not Include

Read this list before comparing our number to anyone else's, because a per-square-foot figure is only meaningful once you know what sits outside it.

- **Land.** Frequently the larger number on a Vancouver project.
- **Design and consultants.** Architectural, structural, geotechnical, survey, energy modelling and interior design.
- **Municipal fees, development cost charges and utility connections.**
- **Landscaping beyond immediate site works** — planting, irrigation, outdoor kitchens, pools.
- **Furniture, appliances beyond the standard package, art and audiovisual systems.**
- **Contingency.** Carry 15–20% on a custom residential build. On a site with unknowns below grade, carry the top of that.

## What BC Requires of a New Home Build

Two requirements sit underneath every legitimate new home in this province, and both are worth confirming before you sign a construction contract.

**A Licensed Residential Builder.** New homes in BC must be built by a builder licensed through BC Housing. The licence is public and checkable.

**2-5-10 Home Warranty insurance.** Two years on labour and materials, five years on the building envelope, ten years on structure. It is mandatory on new homes built by a licensed builder, and it attaches to the licence rather than to a promise — which is exactly why the licence matters.

Beyond those, the **BC Energy Step Code** governs how the envelope and mechanical systems have to perform. On a high-finish build most of what it asks for is specification you would have chosen regardless, so it tends to cost less here than it does on a budget home. One local exception is worth knowing: the City of Vancouver permits under its own building by-law rather than the BC Building Code, so a project inside the city boundary answers to a different rulebook than one in Surrey or Burnaby.

## How the Timeline Runs

Construction on a home in this band runs 14 to 20 months at a typical 4,500 sq ft size, and design and permitting sit ahead of that as their own stretch. The schedule risks specific to luxury work are not the trades — they are procurement. Engineered glazing, stone slabs and drawn millwork all carry lead times that have to be committed to long before they are installed, and a decision deferred on any of the three does not cost you a week, it costs you the lead time.

The practical consequence is that a luxury build rewards front-loaded decision-making more than any other residential project. The owners whose homes finish on schedule are the ones who closed the finish schedule early.

## Building New Against Renovating a High-End Home

Where an existing home has good bones, a substantial renovation can reach a comparable finish level for less than a new build, because the structure, foundation and envelope are already paid for. Where it does not, renovation costs converge on new construction quickly and you inherit the original geometry permanently.

The honest test is whether you would choose the existing floor plan if you were drawing it today. If the answer is no, a renovation buys you a compromise at close to the price of the thing you actually wanted. Our guide to [commercial renovation against new construction](/blog/commercial-renovation-vs-new-construction/) runs the same decision on the commercial side, and the logic transfers.

## Choosing Who Builds It

The distinction that matters at this level is not licensing, it is whether the builder runs a shop-drawing and procurement process rather than a purchasing process. ## Conclusion

A luxury home in Vancouver costs $300–$400 per sq ft to build, and where you land in that band is decided by the site, the glazing, the millwork and the mechanical scope — in roughly that order. The number that ruins budgets is almost never the one in the contract; it is the geotechnical condition nobody paid for a report on, or the millwork package priced from an allowance instead of a drawing. Settle those early and the range narrows fast. We build high-finish residential across the Lower Mainland and would be glad to walk a site with you before you commit to a design.
