## How to Vet Any Contractor Before You Sign

Whoever you shortlist, ask all of these. The answers are more informative than any website, including ours.

1. **What is your BC contractor licence, and can I see a current WorkSafeBC clearance letter?** Both are verifiable in minutes and non-negotiable.
2. **Show me proof of liability insurance and its limit.**
3. **Three references from the last two years, in my build type.** Not your best three ever — your last three.
4. **Who is my site supervisor, how many other jobs will they run at the same time, and will they change?**
5. **How are change orders priced and approved?** Ask to see a blank one.
6. **What contingency is in this number, and what happens to it if it is not used?**
7. **How long do you hold this price, and what are the escalation terms?**
8. **What is in the schedule for permitting, and what happens if the city takes longer?**
9. **How are deficiencies handled at the end, and when does the warranty start?**
10. **Which parts of this are subcontracted, and which are your own crews?**

A contractor who answers all ten without hesitating is showing you how the job will be run. One who is vague on change orders and contingency is showing you that too. Our longer guide, [10 questions to ask a healthcare construction contractor in BC](/blog/how-to-choose-a-commercial-general-contractor/), covers what a good answer to each actually sounds like.

## Comparing the Quotes, Not the Companies

Once you have three numbers, the comparison stops being about brand and starts being about scope. Two quotes for the same clinic are almost never pricing the same work. Check each on mechanical scope, base-building remediation, permits and inspections, whether millwork is drawn or carried as an allowance, contingency, and deficiency and warranty terms. [Commercial tenant improvement cost per square foot](/blog/commercial-tenant-improvement-cost-per-square-foot/) sets out what each build type should cost, so you can see which quote is out of band and ask why.
