# Physiotherapy & Med Spa Clinic Build-Out Guide (BC)

Physiotherapy clinic construction and med spa construction get grouped together because they are often built by the same owners, in the same buildings, sometimes in the same tenancy — and because both fall inside the $150–$320 per sq ft healthcare construction band in BC. But they are close to opposite build types. A physiotherapy clinic wants open floor, ceiling height, impact-rated surfaces and durability. A med spa wants enclosed, quiet, plumbed rooms with a finish level closer to a hotel than a clinic. If you are building either — or both in one space — the decisions that matter are made before the layout is frozen.

> **Key takeaway.** Both build types sit in the **$150–$320 per sq ft** healthcare construction band. Physiotherapy usually lands in the lower half because open rehab floor is inexpensive square footage; med spa lands in the upper half because plumbed treatment rooms and hospitality-grade finishes are not. A 2,000 sq ft space runs roughly $300,000–$640,000 for construction, and the split between open floor and enclosed rooms is what decides where inside that range you land.

## What Each Build Type Actually Needs

| | Physiotherapy clinic | Med spa |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dominant space type | Open rehab and gym floor | Enclosed treatment rooms |
| Plumbing demand | Low — a few sinks | High — sink in most treatment rooms |
| Flooring | Impact-rated, sound-dampening | Cleanable, non-porous, hospitality finish |
| Acoustic priority | Contain noise from the gym floor | Protect privacy in every room |
| Ceiling height | Matters — equipment and overhead work | Standard is fine |
| Ventilation | General comfort plus odour control | Treatment-specific exhaust where required |
| Finish level | Durable and clinical | Closer to hospitality |
| Where it sits in the band | Lower half | Upper half |

## Physiotherapy Clinic Construction

The cost logic of a physiotherapy build is simple: open floor is cheap, walls are expensive. A clinic that is 60% open rehab space costs meaningfully less per square foot than one carved into private treatment rooms, which is why the first useful question is not "how big" but "how much of it is enclosed."

Four things are worth spending on:

**Flooring, chosen zone by zone.** The rehab floor needs impact resistance and sound dampening — dropped weights carry through a slab and into the tenancy below, which is a lease problem as much as a construction one. Treatment rooms need cleanable, non-porous surfaces. One product rarely does both well.

**Acoustic separation between gym and treatment areas.** A physiotherapy clinic generates real noise. Insulated demising walls, sealed ceiling penetrations and solid-core doors on treatment rooms are cheap at framing and impossible afterwards.

**Ceiling height and structural attachment.** Overhead equipment, pulley systems and suspended attachments need real structure and real clearance. Confirm both before signing.

**Accessibility, properly.** A rehab clinic serves patients with mobility limitations by definition. Door widths, washroom clearances, treatment table access and parking are all worth exceeding the minimum on.

## Med Spa Construction

A med spa build is closer to a small hotel with clinical requirements attached. The treatment rooms drive the cost.

**Plumbing to most rooms.** Facials, body treatments and many injectable protocols need a sink in the room. Plumbing to eight treatment rooms in a shell that had two washrooms is a serious mechanical scope, and it is the item most often underestimated.

**Ventilation matched to the treatments offered.** Laser treatments generate plume that needs dedicated exhaust. Rooms where products are mixed or injectables handled need cleanable surfaces, hand hygiene placement and controlled storage. Specify this against CSA Z8000 with the mechanical consultant rather than inheriting whatever the base building provides — our [CSA Z8000 compliance checklist](/blog/csa-z8000-compliance-checklist/) covers what that involves.

**Acoustic privacy in every room.** A client should not hear the consultation next door. This is a demising wall and door specification, decided at framing.

**Lighting on two circuits per room.** Treatment lighting for the practitioner, ambient lighting for the client experience. They are different requirements and both are needed.

**A finish level that survives disinfectant.** This is where med spa builds go wrong most often. Hospitality finishes look right and fail fast against hospital-grade cleaning. Specify materials that meet both the aesthetic and the cleaning protocol.

Where your project sits depends far more on the enclosed-versus-open ratio than on the square footage.

## Building Both in One Tenancy

Combined physio and med spa practices are increasingly common, and they work well commercially — shared reception, shared administration, overlapping clientele. Construction is where the conflict shows up, because the two halves want incompatible things from the same building.

Three rules make it work:

1. **Zone the plan so the gym floor and the treatment rooms do not share a wall.** Put circulation, storage or administration between them.
2. **Run the plumbing scope for the med spa half before finalising the physiotherapy layout.** Drainage has to go somewhere, and that constraint is easier to solve while the open floor is still movable.
3. **Give each half its own flooring, lighting and acoustic specification.** Trying to find one product that satisfies both means neither works properly.

## Conclusion

Physiotherapy clinic construction and med spa construction both live in the $150–$320 per sq ft healthcare band, but they get there by opposite routes — one by keeping the floor open, the other by enclosing and plumbing it. The ratio of open floor to enclosed treatment rooms is the number that actually predicts your budget, and it is worth settling before drawings rather than during pricing.
