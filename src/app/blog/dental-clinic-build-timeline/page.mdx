# Dental Clinic Build Timeline: From Lease Signing to First Patient

Every new dental clinic owner asks the same question early on: how long is this actually going to take? A realistic dental clinic build timeline runs 6–10 months from the day you sign a lease to the day you see your first patient — and the phase that catches most owners off guard is not construction. It is the design, permitting and equipment ordering that happens before a single wall goes up. Most of what stretches a dental clinic construction timeline past ten months happens in those early weeks, not on site. The clinics that hit their opening date are almost always the ones that started ordering long-lead equipment months before construction began.

> **Key takeaway.** A typical dental clinic build timeline runs **6–10 months** from lease signing to opening day, broken into design and permitting (2–4 months), tenant improvement construction (2–4 months), and equipment installation with final inspections (3–6 weeks). The biggest variables are permit turnaround and long-lead dental equipment, both of which should be started as early as possible.

## Phase by Phase

| Phase | Typical duration | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lease negotiation & signing | 4–8 weeks | Space selection, lease terms, tenant improvement allowance negotiated with the landlord |
| Site assessment & test-fit | 2–3 weeks | Contractor and designer assess the shell, confirm plumbing and electrical capacity, produce a preliminary layout |
| Design development | 4–8 weeks | Architectural, mechanical, electrical and plumbing drawings for operatories, sterilization and lab space |
| Landlord approval of TI drawings | 1–4 weeks | Landlord reviews before permit submission — a common bottleneck |
| Permit submission & approval | 4–10 weeks | Municipal building permit, electrical and mechanical permits through Technical Safety BC; longer if health authority input is needed |
| Equipment ordering (runs in parallel) | 12–20 weeks lead time | Chairs, imaging units, sterilization equipment and compressors, ordered as soon as design is final |
| Tenant improvement construction | 8–16 weeks | Framing, plumbing and electrical rough-in per operatory, drywall, flooring, casework |
| Plumbing & electrical rough-in | 2–3 weeks, within construction | Vacuum lines, air lines, dedicated drains and circuits at each chair position |
| Equipment installation | 1–3 weeks | Chairs, imaging, sterilization and compressor systems installed and connected |
| Inspections | 1–3 weeks | Building, electrical, mechanical and fire inspections; health authority walkthrough if applicable |
| Occupancy permit & business licence | 1–2 weeks | Municipality issues occupancy; business licence processed |
| Staff training & soft opening | 1 week | Team trained on the new space before full patient volume |

## What Commonly Causes Delays

Almost every delayed dental clinic timeline traces back to one of a handful of predictable causes:

1. **Permit backlogs.** Building departments in busier Lower Mainland cities take longer during peak submission periods, and a permit quoted at six weeks can stretch with no warning.
2. **Long-lead equipment ordered too late.** Chairs, panoramic units and sterilization equipment frequently carry 12–20 week lead times. Owners who wait until construction starts end up with a finished space and no chairs to put in it.
3. **Landlord delays on TI drawings.** Some landlords review quickly; others sit on drawings for weeks, especially in larger multi-tenant medical or retail buildings.
4. **Scope changes mid-design.** Adding an operatory, a CBCT room or a second sterilization station after drawings are underway resets parts of the design and permitting process.
5. **Inspection scheduling gaps.** If a trade is not ready when an inspector arrives, the next available slot can be one to three weeks out, not next-day.
6. **Utility or structural surprises.** Existing shell plumbing or electrical capacity that does not match what was expected can force design changes after construction has started.

## Where Dental and Medical Timelines Diverge

Dental clinics share a lot of DNA with broader medical office construction — both need specialized plumbing, higher electrical loads, and finishes that meet infection control standards. Dental adds its own wrinkle: chair-side utilities. Every operatory needs its own vacuum line, compressed air line and dedicated drain, which means the rough-in phase for a dental clinic typically takes longer per square foot than a general medical clinic with the same number of exam rooms.

Contractors who have not built dental space before routinely underestimate this phase. It is one of the most common places a timeline slips by two to three weeks without anyone catching it until inspections.

## Conclusion

A realistic dental clinic construction timeline runs 6–10 months from lease signing to first patient, and the clinics that hit that window treat design, permitting and equipment ordering as parallel tracks rather than a strict sequence. Knowing where permit backlogs, landlord approvals and long-lead equipment usually bite gives you a real head start on an opening date you can actually announce.
