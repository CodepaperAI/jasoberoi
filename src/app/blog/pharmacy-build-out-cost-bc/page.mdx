# Pharmacy Build-Out Cost Guide: What BC Pharmacy Owners Need to Know

Pharmacy build-out cost in BC generally runs $180–$250 per sq ft turnkey, which puts a typical 1,500 sq ft community pharmacy at roughly $270,000–$375,000 for construction. That is a narrower band than most healthcare build types, and there is a reason for it: pharmacies vary less in layout than clinics do. What moves the number is not floor area so much as two specific things — how secure the dispensary has to be, and whether you are compounding. Get those two answered early and the rest of your pharmacy construction cost is fairly predictable.

> **Key takeaway.** Budget **$180–$250 per sq ft** for a pharmacy build-out in BC. A 1,000 sq ft pharmacy runs about $180,000–$250,000, a 1,500 sq ft pharmacy about $270,000–$375,000, and a 2,500 sq ft pharmacy about $450,000–$625,000 — construction only. Shelving, point-of-sale, inventory, signage and professional fees sit outside that, and compounding pushes you toward the top of the band.

## Where the Money Actually Goes

A pharmacy budget splits differently from a clinic budget. The plumbing scope is small; the millwork and security scopes are large.

**Dispensary millwork.** The dispensary is the most expensive room per square foot in the building. Workflow counters, shelving systems sized to your inventory model, a will-call area, and the casework around your dispensing robotics if you are using them. This is custom work, and it is where a cheap bid usually cuts.

**Security and controlled storage.** Narcotics storage, controlled access to the dispensary, alarm and camera infrastructure, and a secure perimeter to the standard your licensing requires. Specify this at design, because retrofitting secure storage into finished millwork means rebuilding the millwork.

**The compounding room, if you have one.** A non-sterile compounding area needs separation from general dispensing, dedicated ventilation, cleanable non-porous surfaces and appropriate casework. Sterile compounding is a different order of project altogether and needs to be scoped before you commit to a space.

**Refrigeration and electrical.** Vaccine and medication refrigeration needs dedicated circuits and, in most cases, monitoring and backup considerations. Retail shells are rarely wired for it.

**The customer-facing floor.** Retail-grade flooring, lighting, shelving and a consultation room or private counselling area. This is the part that reads as a pharmacy to a customer, and it competes for budget with everything above.

## Standalone Retail vs. a Unit Inside a Medical Building

Where you put the pharmacy changes the build cost more than most owners expect.

| | Retail conversion | Unit in a medical/dental building |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base building services | Often need upgrading for a healthcare tenant | Usually closer to what you need |
| Electrical capacity | Frequently the binding constraint | Generally adequate |
| Referral proximity | Depends entirely on the trade area | Prescribers already in the building |
| Signage and visibility | Better street exposure | Often limited to building signage |
| Typical build cost | Toward the top of the band | Toward the middle or bottom |
| Lease negotiation | Wider choice of space | Fewer units, less leverage |

Neither is universally better. A high-visibility retail conversion in a strong trade area can be worth the extra construction cost several times over. What matters is pricing the build honestly for the space you are actually considering, rather than applying an average to both.

## What to Verify Before You Sign the Lease

Four questions decide whether your pharmacy build-out lands in the middle of the band or at the top of it:

1. **Is there electrical capacity for refrigeration, equipment and lighting?** Adding service to a retail unit is expensive and slow.
2. **Can the unit be secured to your licensing standard?** Storefront glazing, back-of-house access and after-hours security all affect this.
3. **Can the ventilation support a compounding room?** If compounding is anywhere in your business plan, ask now, not later.
4. **What does the landlord's tenant improvement allowance actually cover?** It is rarely the full build. Our guide to [what a tenant improvement allowance covers](/blog/tenant-improvement-allowance-explained/) sets out where the boundary usually sits, and the [12-point lease checklist](/blog/commercial-lease-for-a-clinic-checklist/) covers the rest.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does a pharmacy build-out cost in BC?** Pharmacy construction in BC generally runs $180–$250 per sq ft turnkey. A typical 1,500 sq ft community pharmacy therefore costs about $270,000–$375,000 for the build-out, before shelving, point-of-sale, inventory and professional fees. A compounding pharmacy sits at the top of that band or above it, because the compounding room carries ventilation and finish requirements a standard dispensary does not.

**What drives pharmacy construction cost the most?** Dispensary security and compounding-room compliance. Secure storage for narcotics, controlled access to the dispensary, and — where compounding is planned — a separate room with its own ventilation, cleanable surfaces and often a certified containment hood are the items that separate a $180 per sq ft pharmacy from a $250 per sq ft one.

**Does a pharmacy need a separate compounding room?** Only if you intend to compound. Non-sterile compounding requires a designated area with appropriate ventilation, cleanable surfaces and separation from general dispensing, following USP 795 principles. Sterile compounding is a substantially larger undertaking involving controlled environments under USP 797 and should be scoped with your consultant before you sign a lease, because most retail shells cannot accommodate it without major mechanical work.

**How long does a pharmacy build-out take?** Most pharmacy build-outs in BC run 3 to 5 months of construction once permits are issued. Permitting sits ahead of that. A pharmacy inside an existing medical or dental building typically moves faster than a standalone retail conversion, because the base building services are usually closer to what a healthcare tenant needs.

**Is a pharmacy cheaper to build than a medical clinic?** Not necessarily. Pharmacy construction runs $180–$250 per sq ft against $150–$260 per sq ft for a medical clinic, so the bands overlap heavily. A pharmacy has less plumbing than a clinic but more millwork, more security infrastructure and more retail-grade finish in the customer-facing area.

**Can a pharmacy be built inside an existing retail unit?** Usually yes, and most community pharmacies are. The three things to verify before signing are electrical capacity for refrigeration and equipment, whether the unit can be secured to the standard your licensing requires, and whether the ventilation can support a compounding room if one is planned. Confirm all three with a building permit application in mind rather than after the lease is signed.

Pharmacy build-out cost is one of the more predictable numbers in healthcare construction, which is good news for anyone modelling a new location. The $180–$250 per sq ft band holds for most community pharmacies in BC, and the two things that move you within it — dispensary security and compounding — are decisions you control and can price before you sign anything.
